Germany · Student engineering work

Werkstudent in Germany: understand the legal work limit before optimising for salary.

A working-student role can be valuable engineering experience, but the old article overstated typical pay, financing effects and job outcomes. Here is the source-checked version.

Reviewed: 11 September 2026Official-source check: Federal Government / BMAS6 min read

What “Werkstudent” means in practice

A Werkstudent role is employment undertaken while you are enrolled as a student. For international students from third countries, immigration-law work limits matter alongside the employment contract and social-insurance rules.

The German Federal Government portal Make it in Germany states that international students may work 140 full days or 280 half days per year. Under the newer rules, an alternative is working in student employment for up to 20 hours per week. Different rules can apply to certain student-assistant activities and individual residence situations, so check your permit and the current official guidance.

Pay: use the legal floor, not a made-up “typical range”

Germany’s statutory minimum wage is €13.90 gross per hour from 1 January 2026. BMAS states that it rises to €14.60 gross per hour on 1 January 2027. Individual Werkstudent roles may pay more depending on company, field, location and experience, but ARVENAIRE no longer publishes a universal €12–€20 range as if it applies to every engineering student.

Gross hourly pay is not take-home pay. Taxes, health insurance and social-security treatment depend on your circumstances. Do not build a study-financing plan from the headline hourly rate alone.

A Werkstudent job does not automatically replace visa financing requirements

Income from employment can matter when proving financial means in some residence-permit situations, but there is no universal rule that a Werkstudent job “removes the need for a blocked account after the first year.” Financing and residence requirements depend on the competent authority and your case. Verify them before making a financial commitment.

The real career value is evidence

The strongest reason to pursue a technical Werkstudent position is not a guaranteed full-time offer. It is the chance to accumulate credible industry evidence: production code, test procedures, PLC work, CAD, embedded systems, validation, documentation, manufacturing processes or robotics tasks that future employers can evaluate.

No honest advisor can claim that one or two years of Werkstudent experience “almost guarantees” a graduate job. It can strengthen your profile substantially, but hiring still depends on the market, language, role fit, work authorisation, performance and the employer’s needs.

How to target engineering roles

  1. Search by task, not title alone. Use keywords such as automation, controls, embedded, test, mechatronics, robotics, production engineering or computer vision.
  2. Match the language of the vacancy. The federal Make it in Germany portal advises applicants to use the job advertisement as the guide for the application language.
  3. Show proof. Link projects, repositories or technical reports that demonstrate the same kind of work the vacancy requests.
  4. Quantify carefully. Use real measurements—cycle time, test coverage, mapping error, latency or hardware constraints—rather than invented performance claims.
  5. Read the contract. Weekly hours, pay, leave and role scope should be clear before you accept.

What to verify before accepting

  • Your residence permit and applicable student work limit.
  • Whether the hours fit your study obligations and current legal framework.
  • Gross pay, expected deductions and health-insurance implications.
  • Whether the role actually contains technical work relevant to your career target.
  • Any university rules that affect your enrollment or programme.
